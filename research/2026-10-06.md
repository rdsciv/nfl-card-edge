## What profit means in this report

PSA’s current US Standard service is **$59.99 per card**, with a $1,000 insured-value limit and **90–100 business days** estimated turnaround. Value and Value Bulk are unavailable. October grading can return after the regular-season attention window; it does not fit the 30–90 calendar-day trade. Faster service costs more. [Official PSA pricing](https://www.psacard.com/services/tradingcardgrading).

The model assumes US eBay non-Store/Starter sports-card fees: **13.25% of item price, collected shipping and buyer sales tax**, plus $0.30 per order at $10 or less or $0.40 above $10. An illustrative 8% tax rate applies to purchases and the buyer’s checkout. Actual location, seller plan and shipping terms can change the result. [Official eBay fee schedule](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822).

For slabs, $6 shipping collected equals $6 actual outbound postage, with $1 packaging. Net proceeds = **0.8569 × sale item price − $2.2586**. Acquisition = **(entry item price + $6 inbound postage) × 1.08**. For cheap Wilson raw, the example uses $1 pass-through postage and $0.25 packaging. These are explicitly assumed costs, not shipping quotes. Returns, paid ads, international fees, grading upcharges, income tax and the value of waiting are excluded.

The entries below are **conditional bid limits**, not verified available bargains. Reference exits use observed prices; downside and upside exits are hypothetical stress cases without probability weights. The ranking needs disciplined acquisition: paying the recent median and reselling at that same median produces a loss after costs.

## Raw-to-PSA 10: the upside is real, the hit rate is not known

I model $59.99 grading plus **$12 allocated insured submission/return shipping and $2 preparation**, totaling $73.99 before acquiring the raw card. Raw inbound postage is assumed $5, with 8% purchase tax. The total cost per attempt is **Maye $198.73, Nix $153.91, Shough $122.59**, using raw item prices $110.50, $69 and $40. The $40 Shough input is one reported sale, not a robust current buy quote. Maye and Shough can exceed Standard’s $1,000 value limit; a hypothetical $20 higher grading fee would turn Shough’s +$17.50 historical-rate sensitivity into −$2.50.

Expected profit = **p(10) × net PSA 10 proceeds + [1 − p(10)] × net failed-grade proceeds − total cost**. The table shows two failure cases: every miss returns PSA 9 (optimistic), or every miss gets a lower salvage value (conservative sensitivity). The Maye/Nix PSA 9 inputs $155/$85 are recent reported medians; Shough’s $100 PSA 9 input is an assumption. Lower salvage values $55/$35/$40 are assumptions, not established grade-8 medians. They make the unknown non-gem distribution visible rather than hide it.

At conservative salvage, break-even gem probabilities are **20.4% Maye, 22.8% Nix, 14.9% Shough**. If the PSA 10 exit falls 40% while grading, they rise to **35.5%, 39.5%, 25.8%**. At a hypothetical 20% hit rate, modeled profit is **−$3.05, −$15.64, +$31.11** respectively; after the 40% price drop all three lose money. Substituting the older observed census fractions yields only about **+$10.84 Maye, +$3.28 Nix and +$17.50 Shough** under conservative salvage. Those are sensitivity calculations, not forecasts.

**Shough is the strongest of these grading sensitivities at a genuinely clean $40 raw entry**, but I would keep it separate from the short-term investment list because efficiency is falling, selection risk is substantial, and grading locks capital for months. Maye’s enormous gem premium does not make indiscriminate raw buying safe. Odunze and Wilson do not need to be manufactured as slabs for the proposed trades; buying an existing grade avoids the unknown hit rate and delay.
